Where do ladder and SVF fit?

Low-pass and high-pass describe what a filter does. Low-pass reduces high frequencies; high-pass reduces low frequencies.

Ladder and SVF describe how a filter is designed. A ladder filter links stages together, often giving a steep low-pass shape. SVF means state-variable filter: a design that can produce low-pass, high-pass, and band-pass responses. Band-pass keeps a middle band of frequencies.

These SVF and ladder examples are simplified working models, not copies of the OP-XY’s algorithms. On the OP-XY, compare the filter choices with the same sound and similar cutoff settings. Listen to the slope and the ringing as you raise resonance. Learn cutoff and resonance first; then explore each model’s character.

What does ADSR mean?

Attack → Decay → Sustain → Release. A note starts, settles, stays held, then ends. Attack, decay, and release are times. Sustain is the level held while you keep the note pressed.

The same shape can control different things: cutoff for the filter envelope, volume for the amp envelope.

How is an LFO different from an envelope?

An envelope follows a note through attack, decay, sustain, and release. An LFO repeats a wave over and over. Here the wave restarts with each note, like a triggered LFO.

This lesson uses a repeating wave as the source and three destinations. On your OP-XY, the Value LFO is a useful place to explore this idea. Other types add random, sensor-driven, or specialized movement.
